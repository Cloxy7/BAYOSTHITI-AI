How the score works

Every drug is described by four groups of numbers, and each group adds a smooth curve to one score:

score = 100 x calibrated probability of logit P = b0 + f(2D similarity) + f(3D similarity) + f(pKa) + f(ESP)

What each group is

  • 2D similarity: how alike the drug's structure is to known drugs that already have this counter-ion published.
  • 3D similarity: the same idea using 3D shape numbers.
  • pKa: the difference between the drug pKa and the ion pKa (the pKa rule). It is a score input and a label, never a filter.
  • ESP: how well the electrostatic surfaces of the two ions complement each other (xTB charges).

How much each group matters

Share of the drop in Hit@10 when the group is taken out. pKa and ESP have confidence intervals that include zero.

Limits

  • The labels are reported pairs, not proven good pairs. A pair nobody reported is not a failed pair.
  • About a quarter of the reported pairs use an ion that no other drug uses. This method cannot suggest a brand-new ion.
  • For a drug you enter yourself, ESP is not computed (the median is used) and pKa is only used if you supply a measured value.
  • Not predicted: melting point, solubility and toxicity of the pair.

Next step

A diffusion model that proposes new ion structures is planned as future work, so that suggestions are not limited to ions already seen in the literature.
